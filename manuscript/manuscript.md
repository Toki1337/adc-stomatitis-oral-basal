## Abstract

**Background:** Stomatitis limits antibody–drug conjugate (ADC) dosing, yet discovery pipelines prioritize untested tumor-over-normal selectivity ratios.

**Methods:** Dual-curated monotherapy stomatitis across 66 ADCs (κ = 0.953, ICC = 0.997; 28 targets; 44 evaluable: 15 numeric, 29 left-censored) linked to oral basal keratinocytes (GSE164241), oral squamous cell carcinoma (OSCC; GSE181919, GSE103322), TCGA, CPTAC, GTEx, HPA (509 DAB sections), and FAERS via Tobit likelihood-ratio (LR), 10,000 permutations, leave-one-target-out (LOTO), nested models, and surfaceome ranking.

**Results:** Oral basal RNA predicted stomatitis (LR = 13.55, β = 0.99, target-cluster bootstrap 95% CI 0.63–3.28, permutation P = 0.0004, ρ = 0.535), persisting without TROP2 (10.72), within topoisomerase-I (7.59) and tubulin ADCs (4.71). Fifteen HPA cell types and 4 GTEx tissues passed q < 0.05 (urothelial/esophageal basal q = 0.044 without TROP2). Oral proteomics (LR = 8.42) and esophageal basal DAB (LR = 8.46, Holm P = 0.029, permutation P = 0.0189; 81/81 settings P < 0.05) predicted stomatitis; oral IHC (0.38), oral DAB (0.15), and skin DAB (0.49) were null. Nested ratios added ≤0.38 LR over basal RNA (P ≥ 0.53), whereas basal RNA added 7.32–13.91 LR (P ≤ 0.0068); uncorrelated axes (ρ = 0.054) ranked *TACSTD2* worst basally (1/28) despite 2/28 selectivity. LOTO predicted unseen targets (concordance C-index 0.752, P = 0.0012; exact ≥15% AUC 0.807 vs. 0.607, P = 0.0046; ρ = 0.597); FAERS oral reporting replicated (β = +1.23, LR P = 0.024, permutation P = 0.0175) with null controls. Across 420 OSCC surface proteins (ρ = 0.714), 47 passed four criteria, 12 had ≤15 TPM across 4 organs (4 across 24), 10 kept ≥6/9 stability, and *CDH3*/*PTPRZ1* 9/9.

**Conclusions:** ADC stomatitis tracks absolute oral basal abundance over selectivity ratios across RNA, proteomics, immunohistochemistry, and pharmacovigilance, guiding OSCC target selection.

**Keywords:** Antibody–drug conjugate; stomatitis; scRNA-seq; basal keratinocytes; OSCC; Tobit regression

## Introduction

Antibody–drug conjugates (ADCs) combine antigen-specific monoclonal antibodies with cytotoxic payloads, yet their clinical therapeutic index is frequently constrained by on-target and off-target normal-tissue toxicities (Esapa et al., 2023; Bosi et al., 2023). Among epithelial adverse events, stomatitis and oral mucositis have emerged as prominent dose-limiting toxicities across next-generation topoisomerase-I and microtubule-disrupting ADCs, necessitating steroid mouthwash prophylaxis, dose interruptions, and treatment discontinuations (Meric-Bernstam et al., 2025). Notably, TROP2-directed ADCs—datopotamab deruxtecan (63.0% any-grade stomatitis on the US prescribing label; U.S. Food and Drug Administration, 2026), SHR-A1921 (64.7%; Chen et al., 2026), and sacituzumab tirumotecan (49.2%; Fan et al., 2026)—exhibit among the highest stomatitis rates in the ADC class.

Computational pipelines for ADC and chimeric antigen receptor (CAR) antigen discovery often combine tumor-versus-normal selectivity metrics with qualitative or atlas-level healthy-tissue screens. For example, Madan et al. (2023) scored both tumor single-cell selectivity and healthy-organ RNA safety across Human Protein Atlas (HPA) and Tabula Sapiens references, while Esapa et al. (2023) reviewed normal-tissue expression qualitatively across approved ADCs. Conversely, a pan-cancer bulk transcriptomic study reported that healthy bulk-tissue target expression only weakly explained clinical ADC adverse events compared with payload mechanisms (Bosi et al., 2023), and single-cell studies demonstrated how bulk tissue pooling masks minority off-tumor cell populations responsible for on-target toxicity (Parker et al., 2020; Zhang et al., 2021). However, none of these pipelines calibrated target abundance directly against quantitative human clinical stomatitis incidence across approved and investigational ADCs. Two quantitative questions remain unresolved: first, when tested in the same nested regression model against human clinical stomatitis, does tumor-to-normal selectivity add predictive safety information beyond absolute target abundance in oral basal keratinocytes? Second, across tissue resolutions and measurement modalities, which oral or stratified squamous representations predict clinical stomatitis?

Here we address these questions using a clinical safety dataset extracted prior to expression linkage across 66 ADCs paired with single-cell transcriptomics of healthy human oral mucosa (Williams et al., 2021), two oral squamous cell carcinoma (OSCC) single-cell cohorts (Puram et al., 2017; Choi et al., 2023), paired bulk RNA and tandem mass tag (TMT) proteomics from The Cancer Genome Atlas Head and Neck Squamous Cell Carcinoma (TCGA-HNSC; Cancer Genome Atlas Network, 2015) and Clinical Proteomic Tumor Analysis Consortium (CPTAC) head and neck squamous cell carcinoma (HNSCC; Huang et al., 2021) cohorts, HPA reference atlases (Uhlén et al., 2015; Karlsson et al., 2021), Genotype-Tissue Expression (GTEx) v8 (GTEx Consortium, 2020), and U.S. Food and Drug Administration Adverse Event Reporting System (FAERS) pharmacovigilance reports via openFDA. Because OSCC originates from stratified squamous oral epithelium, malignant cells share their lineage surfaceome with regenerating healthy basal keratinocytes. We evaluate absolute oral basal abundance versus selectivity ratios head-to-head, test leave-one-target-out out-of-sample calibration, assess pharmacovigilance specificity, and apply calibrated safety thresholds with uncertainty intervals to re-rank the OSCC surfaceome.

## Results

### Study Design and Clinical Stomatitis Cohort

Under a prespecified analytical protocol locked prior to linking clinical outcomes to gene or protein expression, Wendi Dou extracted monotherapy any-grade stomatitis/oral mucositis incidence across 66 clinical-stage or approved ADCs (spanning 39 unique primary target designations / 38 individual genes) from FDA prescribing information and peer-reviewed PubMed Central (PMC) trial full texts (with PubMed abstracts for two left-censored entries), after which Xiang Yu conducted an independent second extraction blinded to gene/protein expression and to Wendi Dou's entries (Figure 1A, Table S1, Table S1a–e, Supplementary References S1–S60). Independent curation showed high inter-extractor agreement before reconciliation across all 66 ADCs (three-category status κ = 0.953, 95% CI 0.878–1.000, 97.0% agreement; evaluability κ = 0.966, 95% CI 0.888–1.000; source-tier κ = 0.839 across all 66 and 0.864 among 43 shared evaluable ADCs; ordinal censoring threshold linear weighted κ = 0.752 across 28 shared censored entries; continuous numeric stomatitis intraclass correlation coefficient ICC(2,1) = 0.997, 95% CI 0.982–1.000, mean absolute error [MAE] = 0.68 percentage points across 14 shared numeric entries; Table S1e). Source-verified adjudication under a unified hierarchy and strict single-agent monotherapy rule retained higher-tier Phase 2 monotherapy tables for rovalpituzumab tesirine (TRINITY, N = 339, <15% cutoff) and trastuzumab rezetecan (HELEN-015, N = 66, 9.1%), while retaining strict single-agent monotherapy FDA-label tables for trastuzumab emtansine (EMILIA) and trastuzumab deruxtecan (DESTINY-Breast06). Across all three dataset versions evaluated in parallel—Wendi Dou's original pre-linkage extraction (n = 44 evaluable, likelihood-ratio [LR] = 13.12, target-permutation P = 0.0004, excluding *TACSTD2* LR = 10.30, leave-one-target-out [LOTO] ρ = 0.643, MAE = 17.5 percentage points), Xiang Yu's unadjudicated second extraction (n = 43 evaluable, LR = 15.09, target-permutation P = 0.0003, excluding *TACSTD2* LR = 12.17, LOTO ρ = 0.533, MAE = 15.6 percentage points), and the adjudicated cohort (n = 44 evaluable, LR = 13.55, target-permutation P = 0.0004, excluding *TACSTD2* LR = 10.72, LOTO ρ = 0.597, MAE = 15.7 percentage points; Table S1c)—primary, sensitivity, and cross-validation findings were consistent. The reconciled cohort yielded 44 evaluable ADCs (representing 28 unique primary targets and 7,673 monotherapy patients; median trial N = 117, range 17–1,365): 15 ADCs reported explicit numeric incidences (9.1% to 64.7%), and 29 listed no oral mucosal term above their adverse-event (AE) table reporting threshold (5% to 20%), forming left-censored observations (Figure 1C). Twenty-two ADCs lacked monotherapy safety tables with N ≥ 15 and were excluded before modeling.

Healthy human oral mucosa single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) from 21 donors (8 buccal mucosa, 13 gingiva; 93,125 post-QC cells; GSE164241; Williams et al., 2021) resolved 6,157 epithelial cells into stratified keratinocyte compartments validated by canonical markers: basal keratinocytes (*KRT5*, *KRT14*, *KRT15*, *COL17A1*, *TP63*; n = 2,876 cells, ≥28 cells per donor), suprabasal keratinocytes (*KRT13*, *KRT4*; n = 1,700 cells), and superficial keratinocytes (*SPRR3*, *CRNN*; n = 968 cells), separated from 613 low-quality or immune/stromal doublet cells (Figure 1B).

### Absolute Oral Basal Expression Predicts Clinical Stomatitis

Across the 44 evaluable ADCs, prespecified payload-adjusted left-censored Tobit regression on the logit scale showed that absolute target expression in healthy oral basal keratinocytes predicted clinical stomatitis incidence (LR = 13.55, likelihood-ratio P = 2.33 × 10⁻⁴; unstandardized β = 0.994 ± 0.245, standardized β = 0.713 per SD; Spearman ρ = 0.535, P = 1.80 × 10⁻⁴; Figure 2B, Table 1, Table S2a). Because multiple ADCs share primary targets (e.g., 6 targeting *ERBB2* and 5 targeting *TACSTD2*), exploratory target-unit inference was anchored across the 28 primary targets: target-level permutation (10,000 resamples permuting basal expression across the 28 primary targets) yielded P = 0.0004 (null median LR = 0.88, 95th percentile = 5.45), and target-cluster bootstrap (2,000 resamples; 1,998 converged) yielded median β = 1.07 (95% CI 0.63–3.28; 0.35% ≤ 0; Table S3a). Alternative basal quantifications behaved similarly, including detection fraction (LR = 16.72, P = 4.34 × 10⁻⁵, ρ = 0.555) and per-donor pseudobulk median (LR = 12.86, P = 3.36 × 10⁻⁴, ρ = 0.518; Table S2a).

Prespecified leave-one-target-out exclusions confirmed that the association did not hinge on any single antigen: dropping each of the 28 targets in turn retained P ≤ 0.0029, with the lowest remaining LR upon excluding *CD276* (n = 3 ADCs, LR = 8.89, P = 0.0029). Removing all five *TACSTD2* (TROP2) ADCs retained LR = 10.72 (n = 39, P = 0.0011, ρ = 0.379), simultaneously removing *TACSTD2* and bispecific *EGFR*;*ERBB3* (izalontamab brengitecan) yielded LR = 6.89 (n = 38, P = 0.0086), and removing all *TACSTD2* and *EGFR* ADCs yielded LR = 6.04 (n = 36, P = 0.0140; Figure 3D, Table S3b). Restricting to solid-tumor ADCs (n = 35) yielded LR = 12.37 (P = 4.36 × 10⁻⁴, ρ = 0.579; excluding *TACSTD2*, n = 30, LR = 9.36, P = 0.0022), and restricting to Phase 2, Phase 3, or FDA-label cohorts yielded LR = 15.84 (n = 28, P = 6.88 × 10⁻⁵, ρ = 0.648).

Within prespecified payload families, oral basal expression remained predictive among topoisomerase-I ADCs (n = 12, LR = 7.59, P = 0.0059, ρ = 0.789; post hoc excluding SN-38, n = 10, LR = 17.54, P = 2.82 × 10⁻⁵, ρ = 0.935) and across tubulin-inhibitor ADCs (n = 25, LR = 4.71, P = 0.030, profile 95% CI 0.16–4.52, ρ = 0.407), while the monomethyl auristatin E (MMAE)-only subset showed a positive non-significant slope (n = 11, β = +0.94, LR = 2.84, P = 0.092, profile 95% CI −0.19 to 3.73). In a descriptive-only post hoc inspection of the four DXd conjugates (not hypothesis-tested because n = 4), clinical stomatitis tracked basal expression monotonically: datopotamab deruxtecan (63.0%, basal 2.29), trastuzumab deruxtecan (15.0%, basal 0.35), patritumab deruxtecan (12.0%, basal 0.22), and ifinatamab deruxtecan (<10%, basal 0.13). Under a post hoc four-level payload specification separating SN-38 from DXd and other topoisomerase-I payloads (four- versus three-level payload LR = 12.20, df = 1, P = 4.79 × 10⁻⁴), the basal-expression LR increased to 25.26 (β = 1.26, target-permutation P = 0.0002), reflecting lower rates for SN-38 ADCs (sacituzumab govitecan 19.0%, ESG401 <20%) than for DXd and other topoisomerase-I TROP2 ADCs (49.2%–64.7%). Across seven post hoc outcome-heterogeneity checks (five subgroup restrictions and two sample-size-weighted specifications; Figure 3D, Table S3b), the slope remained positive in every analysis—all-cause or FDA-label adverse reactions (n = 28, β = +0.92, LR = 7.96, P = 0.0048), treatment-related adverse event (TRAE)-only studies (n = 16, β = +1.04, LR = 5.23, P = 0.0222, Spearman ρ = 0.588), single stomatitis preferred term numeric plus left-censored trials (n = 35: 6 numeric and 29 censored, β = +0.97, LR = 6.58, P = 0.0103), trials with N ≥ 30 (n = 42, β = +0.90, LR = 10.90, P = 0.0010), and sample-size-weighted Tobit (weight = N / mean N: β = +1.15, target-cluster bootstrap 95% CI 0.87–3.55; weight = √N / mean √N: β = +1.06, 95% CI 0.76–3.54)—while the smaller FDA-label-only subset had wider intervals (n = 13, β = +0.74, LR = 2.42, P = 0.120; Spearman ρ = 0.650, P = 0.0162). Data-source sensitivity checks substituting the single stomatitis preferred term for sigvotatug vedotin (11.0% vs. the 22.0% composite oral/pharyngeal mucositis row; LR = 15.47, P = 8.36 × 10⁻⁵) or excluding the two abstract-level censored trials (sofituzumab vedotin and vandortuzumab vedotin; n = 42, LR = 13.40, P = 2.52 × 10⁻⁴) yielded concordant results (Table S3b). Results were insensitive to Spearman censoring imputation at threshold/2, threshold, or 0 (ρ = 0.457–0.535, all P ≤ 0.002), buccal versus gingival site (LR = 13.79 and 13.35), and log₂(counts per 10,000 [CP10K] + 0.1) scaling (LR = 16.22, P = 5.63 × 10⁻⁵).

### Epithelial Resolution Gradient, Quantitative Squamous IHC, and Tissue Specificity

Comparing 15 reference representations across the exact same 44 ADCs (or 32/37 ADCs for protein subsets) and three-level payload covariate revealed that squamous RNA sources—both single-cell keratinocyte fractions and squamous bulk mucosa—as well as continuous normal adjacent tissue (NAT) oral proteomics and quantitative esophageal basal 3,3′-diaminobenzidine (DAB) immunohistochemistry predicted clinical stomatitis, whereas HPA oral mucosa immunohistochemistry (IHC), oral mucosa DAB, and skin DAB did not (Figure 3A, Table 1, Table S2a–b). Within healthy oral mucosa (GSE164241), quiescent non-cycling basal clusters (clusters 0, 1, 13; n = 2,051 cells; LR = 14.25, P = 1.60 × 10⁻⁴) performed comparably to overall basal keratinocytes (LR = 13.55) and numerically above cycling basal clusters 5/6 (n = 825 cells; LR = 11.40, P = 7.34 × 10⁻⁴), indicating that oral mucosal susceptibility reflects basal squamous lineage expression rather than cell-cycle activity alone. Model LR declined from basal keratinocytes (LR = 13.55) through suprabasal (LR = 10.53, P = 0.0012) and superficial keratinocytes (LR = 6.73, P = 0.0095) to whole-mucosa unstratified pseudobulk across all 93,125 cells (LR = 6.09, P = 0.0136; dropping to LR = 0.99, P = 0.319 without TROP2). Squamous bulk mucosa from GTEx esophageal mucosa (LR = 12.19, P = 4.80 × 10⁻⁴) and TCGA-HNSC adjacent normal oral tissue (LR = 11.92, P = 5.56 × 10⁻⁴) retained positive associations, as did CPTAC-HNSCC adjacent normal oral TMT proteomics (n = 32 ADCs, LR = 8.42, P = 0.0037, ρ = 0.447) and independent single-cell normal oral-cavity epithelium from Choi et al. (2023) (n = 199 cells across 3 donors; LR = 6.34, P = 0.0118, ρ = 0.398; pan-HNSCC normal epithelium across 810 cells from 9 donors: LR = 8.14, P = 0.0043; Table 1, Table S2a), whereas semi-quantitative HPA oral mucosa IHC (n = 37 ADCs across 22 targets) showed no univariate association (LR = 0.38, P = 0.536, ρ = 0.166). Across all 14 pairwise resolution comparisons against overall basal keratinocytes, target-cluster bootstrap 95% CIs for ΔLR spanned zero (e.g., whole-mucosa pseudobulk ΔLR = +7.46, 95% CI −0.11 to +21.07; CPTAC NAT proteome ΔLR = +3.98, 95% CI −4.77 to +17.66; HPA skin DAB ΔLR = +9.86, 95% CI −1.16 to +20.67; HPA oral IHC ΔLR = +9.96, 95% CI −2.58 to +22.23; HPA oral DAB ΔLR = +10.19, 95% CI −3.22 to +21.44; Table S2b), indicating overlapping pairwise precision across individual reference modalities at the current sample size.

To quantify histological target protein directly within algorithm-defined basal squamous compartments without manual rescoring, we applied deterministic color deconvolution (Ruifrok and Johnston, 2001) across all 509 primary normal squamous HPA tissue microarray (TMA) sections spanning 28 clinical/benchmark genes (189 oral mucosa, 166 esophagus, and 154 skin cores; *DLL3*, *F3*, *FOLR1*, *GPNMB*, *ROR1*, and *TNFRSF17* lacked HPA squamous IHC; Table S2c–e). Across all nine tissue-by-summary DAB metrics (three tissues × mean basal, 75th-percentile basal, and whole epithelium, designating mean basal DAB as primary; Table S2c), unpigmented non-keratinizing esophageal squamous epithelium (166 sections) tracked oral basal keratinocyte RNA across the 28 imaged genes (ρ = 0.445, P = 0.0177) and predicted clinical stomatitis across all three summary definitions after nine-test LR Holm correction: algorithm-defined mean basal DAB (LR = 8.46, P = 0.0036, 9-test LR Holm P = 0.029, target-permutation P = 0.0189; ΔLR vs. matched oral basal scRNA [LR = 10.35] = +1.89, bootstrap 95% CI −6.19 to +14.39; permutation-based Holm P values are tabulated in Table S2c), 75th-percentile basal DAB (LR = 9.77, P = 0.0018, LR Holm P = 0.016, permutation P = 0.0105), and whole-epithelium DAB (LR = 7.89, P = 0.0050, LR Holm P = 0.035, permutation P = 0.0237; Table 1, Table S2b–c). Esophageal algorithm-defined basal DAB exhibited algorithmic parameter stability across all 81/81 (100.0%) combinations of a four-parameter segmentation grid (median LR = 7.67, range 6.14–9.24; 74/81 [91.4%] P < 0.01; Table S2e) and retained significance when restricted to HPA Enhanced- or Supported-reliability antibodies (excluding Uncertain antibodies flagged for *FGFR2*, *IL3RA*, and *STEAP1*, and Approved-only antibodies; n = 28 ADCs across 14 targets; LR = 8.87, P = 0.0029, target-permutation P = 0.0127; Table S2c). By contrast, archival HPA oral mucosa TMA punches—which contained smaller stratified squamous epithelial fractions than esophageal cores (core median 9.2% [IQR 6.8%–13.1%] vs. 15.6% [11.8%–18.4%]; 28-gene paired median 9.1% vs. 15.0%, paired Wilcoxon P < 0.0001) alongside lower mucosal target staining (*TACSTD2* basal DAB optical density [OD] 0.140 vs. 0.343; suprabasal 0.183 vs. 0.463)—showed no association with stomatitis (mean basal LR = 0.15, P = 0.696, Holm P = 1.00, target-permutation P = 0.770; 0/81 grid settings P < 0.05; remaining null in cores with ≥15% epithelium [29/189 cores across 14 genes, n = 26 ADCs; LR = 0.002, P = 0.967] or epithelial-area weighting [n = 37 ADCs; LR = 0.33, P = 0.567]), as did keratinized skin cores (mean basal LR = 0.49, P = 0.485, Holm P = 1.00; whole-epithelium LR = 1.15, P = 0.284, Holm P = 1.00; Table S2c, Table S2e).

In payload-adjusted Tobit LR scans (Benjamini–Hochberg false discovery rate [BH-FDR] q < 0.05) across the 54 GTEx bulk tissues and an exploratory panel of 155 HPA single-cell types (unadjusted Spearman correlations from prespecified Test 4 are reported in Table S4), 15 HPA cell types and 4 GTEx tissues passed q < 0.05 (Figure 3B–C, Table S4). Ranked strictly by LR across the 155 HPA cell types, the top five were esophageal basal cells (#1: LR = 17.38, q = 0.0027), urothelial cells (#2: LR = 17.15, q = 0.0027), suprabasal keratinocytes (#3: LR = 15.42, q = 0.0035), esophageal suprabasal cells (#4: LR = 15.34, q = 0.0035), and papillary tip epithelial cells (#5: LR = 13.16, q = 0.0061), followed by ocular epithelial cells (#6: LR = 13.14, q = 0.0061) and skin basal keratinocytes (#7: LR = 13.11, q = 0.0061). Across GTEx bulk tissues, only esophageal mucosa (#1: LR = 12.19, q = 0.0259), suprapubic skin (#2: LR = 10.29, q = 0.0353), vagina (#3: LR = 9.14, q = 0.0353), and sun-exposed lower-leg skin (#4: LR = 9.06, q = 0.0353) passed q < 0.05. After excluding the five *TACSTD2* ADCs (n = 39), two HPA cell types remained significant (BH-FDR q < 0.05)—urothelial cells (#1, LR = 12.33, q = 0.0437) and esophageal basal cells (#2, LR = 11.89, q = 0.0437)—with top-five rank composition preserved, whereas no GTEx bulk tissue remained significant at q < 0.05 (GTEx esophageal mucosa still ranked #1 at LR = 6.04, q = 0.386; Table S4). Urothelial epithelium shares basal *KRT5*/*TP63* and epithelial lineage programs with stratified squamous mucosa, consistent with overlapping mucocutaneous antigen expression for agents such as sacituzumab govitecan and enfortumab vedotin, although GTEx bulk bladder did not reach significance (LR = 3.55, q = 0.268).

### Tumor-to-Normal Selectivity Ratios Add No Information Beyond Basal Expression

We next evaluated whether tumor-to-normal selectivity ratios predict protective safety either alone (Figure 2A–D, Table S5a) or nested within joint payload-adjusted models (logit(y) ~ basal + ratio + payload; Figure 3E, Table 2, Table S5b–c). When swapped into single-predictor models, paired bulk TCGA-HNSC oral fold change (n = 30 pairs) was null (LR = 0.016, P = 0.901, β = +0.023, Spearman ρ = −0.022), whereas single-cell log₂(x + 1) tumor-to-normal ratios had weaker LR than absolute oral basal expression and uniformly **positive** (non-protective) slopes: Choi OSCC over oral basal (LR = 3.61, P = 0.0574, β = +0.968), Puram OSCC over oral basal (LR = 6.23, P = 0.0125, β = +1.017), and Choi internal oral-cavity malignant over oral-cavity normal epithelium (LR = 8.01, P = 0.0046, β = +0.656).

Crucially, across nested joint models directly testing incremental information, adding tumor-to-normal ratio to absolute oral basal expression contributed virtually zero explanatory power across all three sources: Choi OSCC ratio added LR = 0.11 (P = 0.742), Puram OSCC ratio added LR = 0.003 (P = 0.958), and TCGA oral paired log₂ fold change (LFC) added LR = 0.38 (P = 0.537; Table 2, Table S5b). Conversely, adding absolute oral basal expression to any selectivity ratio contributed large, statistically significant increments: added LR = 10.05 over Choi ratio (P = 0.0015), added LR = 7.32 over Puram ratio (P = 0.0068), and added LR = 13.91 over TCGA paired LFC (P = 1.91 × 10⁻⁴; Figure 3E). Sensitivity analyses varying the ratio transformation across pseudocounts 1.0, 0.1 (LR = 0.85, P = 0.358), and 0.01 (LR = 0.13, P = 0.722), linear fold ratio (T + 0.1)/(N + 0.1) (LR = 0.03, P = 0.859), and subsets restricted to basally expressed targets (detection ≥ 0.05, n = 27 ADCs across 13 targets; or mean ≥ 0.05 CP10K, n = 29 ADCs across 15 targets) showed no protective ratio effect under any tested scaling (Table S5c): under basal detection ≥ 0.05, absolute expression retained LR = 10.69 (P = 0.0011), whereas single-cell ratios never became protective (β = +0.29 to +1.47 across scales) and TCGA paired LFC remained null (β = −0.10, LR = 0.20, P = 0.653).

Across the 28 primary ADC targets, absolute oral basal expression and the Choi tumor-to-normal ratio were uncorrelated (Spearman ρ = 0.054, P = 0.7842; Figure 2D, Table S5d), placing *TACSTD2* worst for oral basal abundance (rank 1/28, 2.29 log₂ units) despite ranking second-most selective on the tumor-to-normal ratio (rank 2/28, +1.03 log₂ units). Meanwhile, OSCC malignant-cell expression alone (Choi LR = 10.70, Puram LR = 10.88; Table S5a, Table S5d) tracked clinical stomatitis consistent with the correlation between squamous tumor antigen levels and healthy basal keratinocyte expression.

### Leave-One-Target-Out Calibration and Pharmacovigilance Replication

To eliminate target-identity leakage across drugs sharing an antigen (such as five *TACSTD2* and six *ERBB2* ADCs), we used prespecified LOTO cross-validation (implemented with zero-antigen-overlap folds—holding out every training ADC that shares any target gene with the test ADC, including excluding bispecific *EGFR*;*ERBB3* [izalontamab brengitecan] alongside both *EGFR* and *ERBB3* ADCs) as our primary calibration benchmark, with exploratory distribution-free exact-endpoint metrics and 44-fold leave-one-ADC-out (LOO-ADC) reported in Table S6a, Table S6b, and Table S6c (Figure 4A–B). Evaluated without parametric Tobit distributional assumptions across all 451 unambiguously ordered ADC pairs in the full 44-ADC cohort (comparing numeric pairs and pairs in which an uncensored incidence equaled or exceeded a censored trial's reporting upper bound), LOTO improved left-censored concordance index (C-index) from 0.601 for payload class alone to C-index = 0.752 (5,000-resample 28-target permutation P = 0.0012; 0.796 under post hoc four-level payload; Figure 4A, Table S6b). Furthermore, because an adverse-event table reporting all events ≥T% without listing stomatitis entails an exact true incidence <T% with zero censoring ambiguity, 38 of 44 ADCs (86.4%, spanning 26 targets: 10 positive vs. 28 exact true negatives) had exact uncensored binary status for ≥15% stomatitis: LOTO discriminated unseen targets causing ≥15% stomatitis at receiver-operating-characteristic area under the curve (ROC-AUC = 0.807; 0.875 under post hoc four-level payload; exact Mann–Whitney P = 0.0046, target-permutation P = 0.0020; precision–recall area under the curve [PR-AUC] = 0.749) compared with non-significant payload-only predictions at ROC-AUC = 0.607 (P = 0.327; PR-AUC = 0.417; Figure 4B, Table S6b). Likewise, for ≥10% stomatitis across the 35 of 44 exact-status ADCs (79.5%, 23 targets: 14 positive vs. 21 exact true negatives), LOTO improved ROC-AUC from 0.597 (P = 0.345; PR-AUC = 0.519) to 0.747 (P = 0.0152, target-permutation P = 0.0066; PR-AUC = 0.724), and across all 44/44 exact-status ADCs at ≥20% stomatitis (6 positive vs. 38 exact true negatives) yielded ROC-AUC = 0.741 versus 0.675 (Table S6b). Among the 15 numeric ADCs, LOTO out-of-target predictions improved quantitative ranking over payload alone from Spearman ρ = −0.053 (P = 0.850, MAE = 19.2 percentage points) to Spearman ρ = 0.597 (P = 0.0189, MAE = 15.7 percentage points; Figure 4A). Within the 29 left-censored ADCs alone, binary below-threshold placement was identical between the basal-expression model (26/29, 90%) and payload-only baseline (26/29, 90%; one discordant rescue with target expression—telisotuzumab adizutecan [9.1% vs. 21.0% under <20%]—versus one discordant miss on ESG401, exact two-sided McNemar P = 1.00; Figure 4B). Evaluated by exploratory out-of-target Tobit log-scoring on the logit scale (Table S6c), incorporating oral basal expression improved numeric incidence log-density (−2.14 vs. −2.31) with identical censored below-cutoff placement (26/29 vs. 26/29), while overall log-score (−1.14 vs. −1.03) reflected lower censored log-CDF score (−0.61 vs. −0.36) driven by the extreme *TACSTD2* extrapolation on ESG401 (whereas exploratory 44-fold LOO-ADC improved full-cohort log-score over payload alone at −0.79 vs. −0.90; −1.61 vs. −1.93 numeric and −0.37 vs. −0.36 censored). The three LOTO censored misses reflected specific boundary cases: two *CD276* ADCs (ifinatamab deruxtecan and YL201) were predicted at 10.8%, marginally above their 10% threshold, and ESG401 (*TACSTD2*/SN-38, <20% threshold) was extrapolated at 79.7% when all five *TACSTD2* ADCs were held out simultaneously (while post hoc four-level payload modeling lowered LOO-ADC MAE to 8.6 percentage points and resolved ESG401 to 19.0% when SN-38 peers remained in training, yielding 22/29 censored placement under LOTO and 23/29 under LOO-ADC). Exploratory LOO-ADC metrics yielded C-index = 0.732 versus 0.565 across all 44 ADCs, exact ≥15% ROC-AUC = 0.796 versus 0.582, Spearman ρ = 0.461 (P = 0.0836, MAE = 13.2 vs. 17.8 percentage points), and 28/29 (97%) vs. 25/29 (86%) censored placement (3 vs. 0 discordant, exact two-sided McNemar P = 0.25; Table S6c).

Eight of the 15 numeric ADCs had LOTO absolute errors below 10 percentage points; among the seven with larger residuals—apart from three high-incidence *TACSTD2* conjugates examined below (sacituzumab tirumotecan, datopotamab deruxtecan, and SHR-A1921)—the remaining four highlighted pharmacological factors outside three-level payload class: (1) sacituzumab govitecan (observed 19.0% vs. 79.7% out-of-target three-level prediction) uses a hydrolysable CL2A linker releasing SN-38; (2) izalontamab brengitecan (observed 44.6% vs. 12.1%) is a bispecific *EGFR*;*ERBB3* ADC carrying the camptothecin payload Ed-04 on days 1 and 8 every 3 weeks; (3) sigvotatug vedotin (observed 22.0% vs. 3.6%) reflects a composite adverse-event term (oral or pharyngeal mucositis), whereas the single stomatitis preferred term was 11.0%; and (4) gemtuzumab ozogamicin (observed 21.0% vs. 6.9%) reflects non-site-specified mucositis in relapsed acute myeloid leukemia (*CD33* basal expression 0.011).

Across the five *TACSTD2*-directed ADCs sharing identical healthy oral basal expression (2.29 log₂ units) and identical three-level predictions, observed clinical stomatitis spanned <20% for ESG401 and 19.0% to 64.7% across numeric cohorts, rising from 19.0% (sacituzumab govitecan, hydrolysable CL2A linker releasing SN-38; Cardillo et al., 2015) to 49.2% (sacituzumab tirumotecan, Kthiol pyrimidine-thiol linker with belotecan-derivative KL610023; Fan et al., 2026), 63.0% (datopotamab deruxtecan, GGFG tetrapeptide linker releasing DXd; Okajima et al., 2021), and 64.7% (SHR-A1921, salivary gland carcinoma cohort, n = 17; Chen et al., 2026). Similarly, within *ERBB2* topoisomerase-I ADCs sharing identical target abundance (0.346 log₂ units), observed stomatitis ranged from 9.1% (trastuzumab rezetecan, neoadjuvant early breast cancer in HELEN-015) to 15.0% (trastuzumab deruxtecan, metastatic breast cancer in DESTINY-Breast06). Thus, oral basal abundance explains between-target variation across antigens, whereas within-target variation across constructs targeting the same antigen reflects drug-specific and trial-level factors not modeled here, such as payload potency, linker stability, drug-to-antibody ratio, and study population.

In openFDA FAERS pharmacovigilance data across 15 ADCs (12 unique targets) with ≥50 reports excluding 69 co-reported antineoplastic agents (queried November 16, 2026; report denominators spanning 67 to 13,081), payload-adjusted exposure-offset negative-binomial count regression replicated a specific positive association between oral basal expression and oral mucositis/stomatitis reporting (539 oral events; β = +1.23 ± 0.41 log-rate per log₂ basal unit, 95% CI +0.42 to +2.04, rate ratio 3.42; Wald P = 0.0028, likelihood-ratio LR = 5.07, P = 0.0243; 10,000-resample 12-target permutation P = 0.0175; Figure 4C, Table S8). By contrast, all six control toxicities were null under the identical model: prespecified neutropenia (β = +0.30, LR = 0.32, P = 0.572, target-permutation P = 0.713) and peripheral neuropathy (β = −0.27, LR = 0.45, P = 0.500, target-permutation P = 0.686), alongside exploratory cutaneous rash (β = +0.61, LR = 0.91, P = 0.340, target-permutation P = 0.575), ocular toxicity (β = +0.34, LR = 0.13, P = 0.717, target-permutation P = 0.749), interstitial lung disease/pneumonitis (β = −0.58, LR = 2.09, P = 0.148, target-permutation P = 0.325), and diarrhea (β = +0.11, LR = 0.08, P = 0.773, target-permutation P = 0.685; Figure 4C, Table S8). In sensitivity checks not weighting for the 195-fold difference in drug report denominators, unadjusted Spearman correlation was directionally positive (ρ = 0.395, P = 0.145) and equal-weight empirical log-odds OLS (ordinary least squares) yielded a consistent positive association under the identical 10,000-resample 12-target likelihood-ratio permutation (β = +1.01, LR = 4.40, target-permutation P = 0.0482, parametric F P = 0.079; all six control toxicities null at target-permutation P = 0.47–0.95; Table S8). Across four within-class pairwise FAERS contrasts comparing higher- versus lower-basal antigens (Figure 4D), reporting odds ratios (RORs) were consistently above unity: datopotamab deruxtecan (oral basal 2.29; 151/802 reports, 18.8%) versus trastuzumab deruxtecan (basal 0.35; 126/13,081, 0.96%; ROR = 23.85, 95% CI 18.59–30.60), enfortumab vedotin (*NECTIN4* basal 0.19; 57/3,627, 1.57%) versus brentuximab vedotin (*TNFRSF8* basal 0.00; 33/5,331, 0.62%; ROR = 2.56, 95% CI 1.67–3.94), enfortumab vedotin versus polatuzumab vedotin (*CD79B* basal 0.007; 9/1,208, 0.75%; ROR = 2.13, 95% CI 1.05–4.31), and non-significantly for trastuzumab emtansine (basal 0.35; 29/4,369, 0.66%) versus mirvetuximab soravtansine (*FOLR1* basal 0.00; 5/1,706, 0.29%; ROR = 2.27, 95% CI 0.88–5.88). Separately, the same-target FAERS comparison between datopotamab deruxtecan (18.8%) and sacituzumab govitecan (82/5,477, 1.50%; both targeting *TACSTD2*) yielded an oral ROR of 15.26 (95% CI 11.52–20.21; Figure 4D). ### OSCC Surfaceome Coupling and Exploratory Target Re-Ranking

From reviewed human UniProt entries (n = 20,429), we curated 3,152 surfaceome proteins containing transmembrane helices or glycosylphosphatidylinositol (GPI) anchors alongside signal peptide or cell-membrane annotation (capturing all 38 clinical ADC target genes), of which 1,997 were quantified across all three single-cell cohorts after harmonizing the legacy symbol *PVRL4* to *NECTIN4* across single-cell and TCGA matrices, and 420 were detected in ≥25% of OSCC malignant cells in both Choi (GSE181919; 1,376 malignant cells, 10 patients) and Puram (GSE103322; 1,427 malignant cells, 15 patients; Table S7). Across these 420 OSCC surface proteins, malignant-cell abundance correlated tightly with healthy oral basal keratinocytes (Spearman ρ = 0.714 in Choi; ρ = 0.582 in Puram; Figure 5A). Evaluated on the same shifted log scale as our primary exposure, only 6.2% (26/420) achieved a tumor-minus-basal difference Δlog₂(CP10K + 1) ≥ 1.0 in Choi (4.0%, 17/420 requiring ≥1.0 in Choi and ≥0.5 in Puram) and 0.7% (3/420) reached ≥2.0 (0.0% dual-cohort). On the linear fold scale with 0.1 CP10K pseudocount, 17.9% (75/420) reached ≥2-fold tumor enrichment in Choi (11.4%, 48/420 dual-cohort; 26.9%, 113/420 unshifted raw ratio ≥ 2). Across all 420 OSCC targets, median predicted topoisomerase-I stomatitis was 10.0% (IQR 8.8%–13.0%); 12/420 high-basal non-candidate surface proteins exceeded the maximum clinical training expression (2.29 log₂ units), whereas 0/47 passing candidates lay in the extrapolation region (Table S7).

We then conducted an exploratory four-criterion re-ranking: (1) ≥50% malignant-cell detection in both Choi and Puram; (2) predicted topoisomerase-I stomatitis ≤12%; (3) paired tumor RNA upregulation in TCGA-HNSC oral pairs (n = 30 pairs, mean LFC > 0 and ≥60% pairs up); and (4) paired protein upregulation in CPTAC-HNSCC oral TMT proteomics (n ≥ 15 pairs, mean LFC > 0 and ≥50% pairs up). Forty-seven of 420 targets (11.2%) passed all four criteria (Figure 5C, Table 3, Table S7), and 12 additionally maintained GTEx median expression ≤15 transcripts per million (TPM) across the four-organ core panel (heart left ventricle, liver, kidney cortex, tibial nerve): *CDH3*, *PTPRZ1*, *PTK7*, *FAT2*, *FZD6*, *DSG2*, *PTPRK*, *ITGA2*, *CDCP1*, *IL1RAP*, *IGSF3*, and *ATP2B1*. Expanding to a 24-organ extended panel (the four core organs plus 13 brain regions and 7 non–central nervous system [CNS] systemic organs: lung, stomach, transverse colon, terminal ileum, pancreas, spleen, and whole blood) showed organ-specific liabilities hidden by the four-organ screen (Table 3, Table S7): 4 of the 12 candidates maintained ≤15 TPM across all 24 extended tissues (*CDH3* [10.61 TPM in spleen; 54-tissue GTEx max 50.15 TPM in ovary], *CDCP1* [12.99 TPM in colon; 54-tissue max 49.23 TPM in esophageal mucosa], *IGSF3* [11.91 TPM in kidney; 54-tissue max 36.26 TPM in esophageal mucosa], and *IL1RAP* [13.57 TPM in liver across all 54 tissues]), whereas *PTK7* (38.88 TPM in lung), *DSG2* (37.53 TPM in transverse colon), *FZD6* (21.67 TPM in lung), *ITGA2* (22.31 TPM in lung), and *PTPRK* (19.77 TPM in stomach) showed moderate visceral RNA, and *FAT2* (223.87 TPM in cerebellum; 0.62 TPM across non-CNS extended organs), *ATP2B1* (77.64 TPM in frontal cortex; 19.41 TPM in lung across non-CNS extended organs), and *PTPRZ1* (70.16 TPM in amygdala; 7.31 TPM across non-CNS extended organs) showed CNS expression (full 54-tissue GTEx maxima for all 420 proteins are reported in Table S7). For CNS-expressed antigens (*PTPRZ1*, *FAT2*, *ATP2B1*), systemic IgG ADCs have limited blood–brain barrier penetration across intact endothelium, although CNS exposure still requires dedicated toxicology evaluation in patients with brain metastases or compromised blood–brain barrier integrity. Among the remaining 35 winners excluded by >15 TPM in the four-organ panel (Table S7), UniProt topology annotations also flag predominantly trans-Golgi-cycling *TGOLN2* (max four-organ 118.0 TPM, extended 119.6 TPM) and proteins with short C-terminal extracellular segments (*PLSCR1*, 13 residues, 44.2 TPM; *MYOF*, 15 residues, 41.3 TPM) as requiring surface-epitope validation. Notably, across all 12 four-organ-low candidates, coherent full-cohort cluster-bootstrap 95% prediction intervals (PIs) were wide (spanning 0.6%–44.6% around point estimates of 8.8%–11.6%, consistent with the 15.7 percentage-point LOTO numeric MAE; separate LOTO sensitivity intervals for trained targets are provided in Table S7), so overlapping prediction intervals preclude fine numerical ranking among candidates inside this low-basal tier. When threshold stability was tested across a 3 × 3 exploratory grid (stomatitis 10%/12%/15% × organ threshold 10/15/30 TPM), under the four-organ panel *CDH3* (10.0%, max 3.54 TPM) and *PTPRZ1* (8.8%, 7.31 TPM) passed all 9/9 combinations while 10/12 achieved ≥6/9 stability (including *PTK7* [10.3%, 7.45 TPM] and *FAT2* [10.4%, 0.62 TPM], which missed only ≤10% stomatitis by 0.3–0.4 percentage points); under the extended 24-organ grid, *CDH3* and *IL1RAP* retained 6/9 stability and *CDCP1* and *IGSF3* retained 4/9 stability (Figure 5C, Table 3). ## Discussion

By testing absolute basal-keratinocyte target abundance directly against tumor-to-normal selectivity ratios on an identical 44-ADC clinical safety cohort curated independently in duplicate, this study demonstrates quantitatively that human clinical ADC stomatitis tracks antigen abundance in healthy oral basal epithelium rather than tumor-to-normal fold selectivity. In nested regressions adjusting for payload class, tumor-to-normal ratios from two independent OSCC single-cell cohorts and paired TCGA-HNSC oral cavity samples added ≤0.38 to the likelihood-ratio statistic (all P ≥ 0.53), whereas absolute oral basal expression added 7.32–13.91 LR (all P ≤ 0.0068). Across 28 primary ADC targets, the absolute safety axis and selectivity ratio axis are uncorrelated (ρ = 0.054, P = 0.7842), illustrating how *TACSTD2* ranks among the most tumor-selective antigens on ratio metrics (rank 2/28) while carrying the highest oral basal abundance (rank 1/28) and high clinical stomatitis rates across most TROP2-directed ADCs.

Cross-atlas and histological protein comparisons delineate which normal-tissue representations retain a stomatitis association across bulk, single-cell, and histological modalities. Within oral and stratified squamous epithelia, positive associations span single-cell basal keratinocytes—in both quiescent (LR = 14.25) and cycling basal subclusters (LR = 11.40)—as well as squamous bulk mucosa (GTEx esophageal mucosa LR = 12.19; TCGA normal oral LR = 11.92), continuous adjacent normal oral TMT proteomics (LR = 8.42), and deterministic rgb2hed DAB optical density within algorithm-defined non-keratinizing esophageal basal epithelium (LR = 8.46, 9-test LR Holm P = 0.029, target-permutation P = 0.0189; LR = 9.77 at the 75th percentile; LR = 8.87 across Enhanced/Supported antibodies; algorithmic parameter stability P < 0.05 across all 81/81 tested segmentation settings in Table S2e). By contrast, unstratified whole-mucosa single-cell pseudobulk loses significance without *TACSTD2* (LR = 0.99, P = 0.319), and archival HPA oral mucosa IHC (ordinal LR = 0.38; DAB LR = 0.15, amidst sparser epithelial sampling and attenuated mucosal staining in oral TMA punches) and epidermal skin DAB (LR = 0.49) were non-significant (Table S2c). Pairwise target-cluster bootstrap 95% confidence intervals for ΔLR against oral basal keratinocytes span zero across all 14 reference comparisons, indicating that resolving finer rank order among individual modalities will require larger clinical ADC cohorts. Furthermore, ratio failure persists across log pseudocounts (1.0, 0.1, 0.01), linear scaling, and basal-expressed target subsets; removing log compression simply collapses the *TACSTD2*-driven positive slope to null without revealing protective ratio behavior. Prior pan-cancer bulk analyses observed weak normal-tissue associations across heterogeneous tumor/toxicity pairs compared with payload activity (Bosi et al., 2023); here, focusing specifically on a defined clinical epithelial toxicity against healthy squamous basal progenitors isolates a consistent, quantitative target-abundance signal across RNA, adjacent-normal oral proteomics, quantitative esophageal basal immunohistochemistry, and post-marketing pharmacovigilance after adjusting for or stratifying within payload classes. Payload-adjusted FDR-corrected scans further show that specificity concentrates in stratified squamous and related urothelial epithelia—comprising all four FDR-significant GTEx bulk tissues (esophageal mucosa, sun-exposed and non-sun-exposed skin, and vagina) and holding the top four ranks (esophageal basal #1, urothelial #2, suprabasal and esophageal suprabasal keratinocytes #3–4) and 7 of 15 FDR-significant HPA cell types (including skin basal and ocular epithelia, with the remaining 8 representing other basal/myoepithelial, submucosal glandular, and renal tubular epithelia)—pointing toward shared basal/mucosal antigen programs worth evaluating in bladder and ocular ADC toxicities, with urothelial cells (#1) and esophageal basal cells (#2) remaining the sole FDR-significant cell types (BH-FDR q = 0.044) after excluding *TACSTD2*.

In oral cavity squamous cell carcinoma, malignant transformation preserves the squamous surfaceome, generating tight coupling with healthy oral basal keratinocytes (ρ = 0.714 in Choi; ρ = 0.582 in Puram across 420 OSCC surface proteins) and leaving only 6.2% of OSCC surface proteins with Δlog₂(CP10K + 1) ≥ 1.0 (17.9% linear fold ≥ 2 under pseudocount 0.1). Re-ranking along the calibrated absolute axis combined with clinical multi-omic criteria shifts priority away from high-basal clinical and atlas-screened targets—including *TACSTD2*, *COL17A1*, *DSC3*, *DSG3*, *CD44*, and *GPNMB*—toward lower-basal targets exemplified under the four-organ core panel by the two 9/9 grid-stable candidates *CDH3* and *PTPRZ1* (with *PTPRZ1* also showing low esophageal basal DAB optical density at 0.069 OD), eight additional ≥6/9 four-organ-stable candidates (*PTK7*, *FAT2*, *FZD6*, *DSG2*, *PTPRK*, *ITGA2*, *CDCP1*, and *IL1RAP*), *IGSF3* (4/9 stability across both the four-organ and extended 24-organ panels), *ATP2B1* (4/9 four-organ, 0/9 extended), and clinical benchmark *F3* (tisotumab vedotin). Extending GTEx screening to 24 systemic and CNS organs narrows the ≤15 TPM subset from 12 to 4 candidates (*CDH3*, *CDCP1*, *IGSF3*, *IL1RAP*, with *CDH3* and *IL1RAP* retaining 6/9 extended-grid stability) and flags extra-core visceral expression for *PTK7* (lung) and *DSG2* (colon) alongside CNS expression for *PTPRZ1* (amygdala 70.16 TPM), *ATP2B1* (frontal cortex 77.64 TPM), and *FAT2* (cerebellum 223.87 TPM), where intact blood–brain barrier limits systemic IgG ADC penetration but CNS disease requires safety vetting. Candidate selection nevertheless requires target-specific triage: *CDH3* and *PTK7* already have clinical ADC precedent and strong paired TCGA/CPTAC protein upregulation, *FAT2* shows minimal bulk CPTAC protein elevation (+0.02 LFC) despite independent PDX N-glycoproteomic nomination (Govindarajan et al., 2026), and predominantly intracellular-cycling or short-tail winners (*TGOLN2*, *PLSCR1*, *MYOF*) require experimental surface-epitope validation. Moreover, off-tumor ADC toxicity is not universally receptor-mediated: mirvetuximab soravtansine causes corneal keratopathy (Hendershot et al., 2023) although folate receptor-α (FRα, encoded by *FOLR1*) is absent from human corneal tissue (Matulonis et al., 2019), consistent with target-independent macropinocytic uptake of ADCs by corneal epithelial cells (Zhao et al., 2018), whereas oral *FOLR1* basal RNA is 0.00 and neither mirvetuximab soravtansine nor farletuzumab ecteribulin lists oral events above 10%.

**Limitations:** Although only 15 of 66 ADCs reported explicit numeric stomatitis incidences alongside 29 threshold-censored trials, distribution-free zero-antigen-overlap LOTO confirmed unseen-target discrimination without parametric censoring assumptions across both all 44 ADCs (left-censored C-index = 0.752, P = 0.0012) and the 38 of 44 ADCs with exact uncensored binary status at ≥15% stomatitis (ROC-AUC = 0.807 vs. 0.607 payload-only, P = 0.0046); nevertheless, clinical AE definitions varied across sources (TRAE vs. all-cause, grouped vs. preferred terms), and smaller homogeneous subsets (FDA-label-only n = 13, P = 0.120; MMAE-only n = 11, P = 0.092) showed positive slopes without reaching formal LR P < 0.05. Because primary exposure is defined solely by target-gene expression and three broad payload classes, the model predicts a nonzero conditional median at zero basal expression (7.4% for topoisomerase-I, 3.8% for tubulin, and 8.6% for DNA-damaging payloads) and cannot distinguish different ADCs sharing the same antigen (such as datopotamab deruxtecan versus sacituzumab govitecan on *TACSTD2*, or trastuzumab deruxtecan versus trastuzumab rezetecan on *ERBB2*) without incorporating drug-specific linker stability, payload potency, drug-to-antibody ratio, and study population. Out-of-target LOTO numeric point-rate MAE remains substantial (15.7 percentage points), producing wide coherent full-cohort 95% prediction intervals (0.6%–44.6% across the 12 four-organ-low passing candidates) that prohibit fine ranking among low-basal candidates; payload linker chemistry and schedule also add variance (splitting SN-38 reduced LOO-ADC MAE to 8.6 percentage points and raised LOTO ≥15% AUC to 0.875, though worsening binary censored cutoff placement), and prospective validation in newly emerging monotherapy ADC cohorts remains warranted. In openFDA FAERS across 15 ADCs, both exposure-offset negative-binomial count modeling (LR = 5.07, P = 0.0243; 12-target permutation P = 0.0175) and equal-weight empirical log-odds OLS evaluated under the identical 12-target permutation (β = +1.01, target-permutation P = 0.0482; parametric OLS P = 0.079) replicated specific oral disproportionality with null controls, whereas unadjusted Spearman correlation ignoring payload class was non-significant (P = 0.145), and spontaneous reporting remains subject to reporting bias and lacks exact patient-exposure denominators. Finally, healthy oral and esophageal donors were untreated individuals, transcript abundance does not directly measure receptor internalization kinetics, and histological DAB quantification used automated algorithm-defined epithelial and basal segmentation on HPA TMA cores rather than pathologist-annotated prospective oral biopsies (with esophageal algorithmic basal DAB stability confirmed across all 81 tested segmentation settings).

In summary, calibrating ADC safety directly against human clinical stomatitis outcomes identifies absolute target abundance in healthy oral basal keratinocytes—corroborated by adjacent-normal oral proteomics, quantitative esophageal basal immunohistochemistry, and post-marketing pharmacovigilance—rather than tumor-to-normal selectivity ratios as the key predictor of clinical mucosal toxicity, providing quantitative safety bounds for oral oncology target prioritization.

## Methods

### Prespecified Protocol vs. Exploratory and Post Hoc Analyses

Consistent with the locked analysis plan finalized before linking clinical outcomes to expression data, prespecified elements comprised: (1) primary clinical monotherapy stomatitis/oral mucositis incidence with source hierarchy (FDA label > Phase 3 > Phase 2 > Phase 1) and left-censoring rules below AE table reporting thresholds; (2) secondary FAERS oral disproportionality excluding co-reported antineoplastic agents and two prespecified FAERS negative controls (neutropenia and peripheral neuropathy); (3) primary predictor definition as log₂(mean CP10K + 1) across healthy oral basal keratinocytes (GSE164241; taking the maximum across bispecific target genes and mapping *CD44v6* to *CD44*), alongside four replication predictors (Choi GSE181919 normal oral keratinocytes, HPA oral mucosa IHC, TCGA-HNSC adjacent-normal oral bulk RNA, and CPTAC-HNSCC adjacent-normal oral proteomics); (4) primary tests—Spearman correlation under threshold/2, threshold, and 0 imputations (Test 1), three-level payload-adjusted logit Tobit regression with LOTO evaluation (Test 2, implemented with zero-antigen-overlap folds), within-payload contrasts across power-feasible subsets (Test 3 planned DXd, MMAE, DM1/DM4, and SN-38 contrasts; DM1/DM4 n = 7 [1 numeric], SN-38 n = 2 [1 numeric], and DXd n = 4 [3 numeric, inspected descriptively] had too few numeric events for regression, so topo-I, tubulin, and MMAE models were fitted), the 54-tissue GTEx Test 1 Spearman specificity scan (Test 4, Table S4, extended with payload-adjusted Tobit LR in Figure 3C), and FAERS replication of Tests 1–2 (Test 5, implemented via co-reported antineoplastic exclusion list rather than separate drug-characterization suspect-role filtering); and (5) Part 2 OSCC tumor-versus-basal window profiling with DepMap evaluation, with complete plan-to-implementation mapping summarized in Table S3c. Analyses introduced after initial outcome–expression linkage are explicitly designated as exploratory—namely independent second extraction by Xiang Yu, dual-extractor audit, and consensus adjudication across all 66 ADCs; ### Blinded Dual Clinical Stomatitis Data Extraction and Adjudication

Monotherapy any-grade stomatitis/oral mucositis incidence across 66 clinical-stage or approved ADCs (spanning 39 primary target entries) was originally extracted by Wendi Dou prior to linking clinical rows with expression matrices (Table S1a), followed after initial linkage by an independent second extraction by Xiang Yu blinded to gene/protein expression matrices and Wendi Dou's entries (Table S1b), following a source hierarchy prioritizing FDA prescribing information pooled or pivotal monotherapy safety tables (openFDA label API; Kass-Hout et al., 2016) over peer-reviewed PMC full-text Phase 3, Phase 2, or Phase 1 reports (with PubMed abstracts used when PMC provided no full text for two entries left-censored at 20%, whose abstracts listed only AEs >20%; Table S1, Supplementary References S1–S60). When sources used grouped stomatitis terms (e.g., stomatitis including mouth ulceration, aphthous ulcer, mucosal inflammation, or composite table rows such as oral or pharyngeal mucositis when no separated stomatitis row was tabulated, evaluated separately in single-term sensitivity checks), the tabulated incidence was recorded; otherwise, the maximum incidence across individual oral terms was recorded without summing. When no oral mucosal term appeared in an AE table, incidence was recorded as left-censored below the table's explicit reporting cutoff (e.g., 5%, 10%, 15%, or 20%, including the explicit ≥10% treatment-emergent adverse event cutoff verified in PMC6797631 Table 2 for aprutumab ixadotin). Discrepancies were adjudicated by consensus between Wendi Dou and Xiang Yu directly against primary FDA labels and PMC XML tables under a unified rule: source tier (FDA label > Phase 3 > Phase 2 > Phase 1) took precedence first; within the same tier, strict single-agent monotherapy without concurrent radiotherapy or systemic co-therapy took precedence over regimens permitting concurrent therapy, followed by advanced/metastatic over early-stage settings, recommended Phase 2 dose cohorts over pooled dose-escalation cohorts, and larger sample size N (Table S1d). Inter-extractor agreement prior to adjudication was quantified across all 66 ADCs using unweighted Cohen's κ (status, evaluability, source tier), linear/quadratic weighted κ (ordinal censoring thresholds), and two-way random-effects absolute-agreement ICC(2,1) and ICC(2,k) for shared numeric incidences (Table S1e), and primary Tobit and LOTO analyses were evaluated in parallel across Wendi Dou's original dataset, Xiang Yu's unadjudicated dataset, and the adjudicated cohort (Table S1c). Twenty-two ADCs lacking monotherapy tables with N ≥ 15 were excluded prior to modeling, leaving 44 evaluable ADCs across 28 primary targets (15 numeric, 29 left-censored).

### Single-Cell, Bulk RNA, Proteomic, Quantitative IHC, and Reference Atlas Processing

Healthy human oral mucosa scRNA-seq (GSE164241; Williams et al., 2021) was restricted to healthy buccal mucosa (n = 8) and gingiva (n = 13) donors (113,253 barcodes), retaining 93,125 cells with 300–6,999 detected genes and <20% mitochondrial unique molecular identifiers via Scanpy (Wolf et al., 2018). Counts were CP10K-normalized and log1p-transformed for principal component analysis (40 principal components on 3,000 highly variable genes [HVGs]) and Leiden clustering (resolution = 1.0; Traag et al., 2019). Epithelial clusters (n = 6,157 cells expressing *KRT5*, *KRT14*, *KRT15*, *KRT13*, *SPRR3*) were re-embedded across 2,000 batch-aware HVGs, harmonized across donors with Harmony (Korsunsky et al., 2019), and subclustered (resolution = 0.6) into basal (clusters 0, 1, 13, 5, 6; n = 2,876 cells; further split into quiescent clusters 0, 1, 13 [n = 2,051] and cycling clusters 5, 6 [n = 825]), suprabasal (clusters 2, 3, 9; n = 1,700), superficial (clusters 4, 8, 11; n = 968), and excluded doublet/low-quality (clusters 7, 10, 12, 14; n = 613) compartments. Summary abundance was computed from raw CP10K as log₂(mean CP10K + 1), detection fraction (CP10K > 0), and per-donor medians (donors with ≥20 compartment cells), alongside whole-mucosa pseudobulk across all 93,125 cells.

To quantify normal squamous protein abundance deterministically without manual visual rescoring, all 509 available HPA v26.0 normal squamous IHC TMA images spanning 28 clinical/benchmark genes (189 oral mucosa, 166 esophagus, 154 skin; downloaded November 17, 2026 from v26.proteinatlas.org / proteinatlas.org; Uhlén et al., 2015) were converted from RGB (4× spatial downsampling) to optical density and projected onto Hematoxylin–Eosin–DAB vectors via the inverse color-deconvolution matrix (Ruifrok and Johnston, 2001; Table S2c–e). Default segmentation parameters—fixed prior to modeling on the adjudicated cohort—separated tissue pixels (mean grayscale intensity < 236) and lumen/border background components (>180 px), applied an adaptive cellularity score (Gaussian-smoothed Hematoxylin + 0.35 × DAB, σ = 5.0; threshold = max[58th tissue quantile, 0.03], keeping connected components ≥300 px after morphological opening/closing) to isolate squamous epithelium from lamina propria stroma, and used Euclidean distance transforms to define the algorithm-defined basal region of interest (relative stroma-to-lumen distance ratio ≤ 0.30 and stroma distance ≤ 18 px). Gene-level mean and 75th-percentile basal DAB and whole-epithelium DAB optical densities were averaged across available cores (and separately across Enhanced/Supported antibodies only), with Holm multiplicity correction across all nine tissue-by-summary combinations reported for both LR and target-permutation P values (Table S2c). Core-level epithelial fraction comparisons were paired across the 28 imaged genes via two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank tests, and algorithmic segmentation parameter sensitivity was evaluated across an 81-combination grid varying epithelial quantile (50th/58th/66th), distance ratio (0.25/0.30/0.35), stroma distance (14/18/22 px), and tissue grayscale threshold (228/236/244; Table S2e).

OSCC scRNA-seq cohorts comprised Choi et al. (2023) (GSE181919; 1,376 malignant cells from 10 human papillomavirus [HPV]-negative oral cavity tumors and 199 normal oral-cavity epithelial cells from 3 donors, CP10K-normalized, alongside all 810 normal HNSCC epithelial cells from 9 donors as an auxiliary benchmark in Table S2a) and Puram et al. (2017) (GSE103322; 1,427 primary malignant cells [1,412 from 15 patients and 15 from a pooled multi-patient plate], inverting published E = log₂(TPM/10 + 1) to CP10K scale via 10 × (2^E − 1) / 100). Bulk oral cavity datasets comprised TCGA-HNSC (Cancer Genome Atlas Network, 2015; accessed via University of California Santa Cruz [UCSC] Xena, Goldman et al., 2020, and National Cancer Institute Genomic Data Commons [NCI GDC], Heath et al., 2021; HiSeqV2 log₂(RNA-Seq by Expectation-Maximization + 1) filtered to oral cavity subsites: 315 primary tumors, 30 paired adjacent normals) and CPTAC-HNSCC oral cavity (Huang et al., 2021; accessed via LinkedOmics, Vasaikar et al., 2018, and PDC000221: 49 tumors, 23 paired TMT proteomics normal adjacent tissues, 24 paired RNA-seq NAT). Reference atlases comprised GTEx v8 median TPM (54 tissues; GTEx Consortium, 2020), HPA v26.0 Single Cell Type Atlas normalized counts per million (nCPM; 155 cell types; Karlsson et al., 2021), HPA v26.0 normal oral mucosa IHC (max numeric score 0–3 per gene; Uhlén et al., 2015), and DepMap 24Q4 CRISPR Chronos essentiality across 1,178 cell lines (1,523 common-essential genes; Tsherniak et al., 2017; Dempster et al., 2021). Legacy symbol *PVRL4* was harmonized to *NECTIN4* (HUGO Gene Nomenclature Committee) in all expression matrices.

### Statistical Modeling, Target-Level Inference, and Cross-Validation

Stomatitis incidence was modeled on the logit scale (logit(clip(y / 100, 0.001, 0.999))) using left-censored Tobit maximum-likelihood regression fitted with analytic gradients via bounded BFGS in SciPy (Virtanen et al., 2020), where expit(Xβ) × 100 represents the conditional median predicted incidence:

$$\text{logit}(y_i) = \beta_0 + \beta_{\text{tub}}\,\mathbb{I}[\text{tubulin}_i] + \beta_{\text{DNA}}\,\mathbb{I}[\text{DNA}_i] + \beta_x\,x_i + \varepsilon_i, \quad \varepsilon_i \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2)$$

with topoisomerase-I payloads (DXd, SN-38, topo1-other) as reference, tubulin payloads (MMAE, MMAF, DM1, DM4, auristatin-other, eribulin, hemiasterlin), and DNA-damaging payloads (PBD, calicheamicin, duocarmycin, IGN)—yielding 5 fitted model parameters (intercept, two payload indicators, exposure slope, and log error SD) applied across 21 evaluated oral/squamous/reference specifications (Table 1, Table S2a). Numerically uncensored observations contributed Gaussian log-density and left-censored observations contributed Gaussian log-CDF at their reporting threshold. Standard errors used central-difference Hessians, confidence intervals in unweighted sensitivity checks used exact profile likelihood (re-optimizing nuisance parameters across fixed slope grids), sample-size-weighted Tobit fits (weights N / mean N and √N / mean √N) used 2,000 target-cluster bootstrap replicates for 95% confidence intervals (omitting likelihood-ratio χ² P values because weighted pseudo-likelihood does not follow χ²(1)), and target-level inference evaluated 10,000 target-level permutations and 2,000 target-cluster bootstrap replicates (seed = 1).

Head-to-head axis testing swapped single predictors identically across the 44 ADCs and compared nested joint models (logit(Y) ~ basal + ratio + payload, df = 1 likelihood-ratio additions) across Choi, Puram, and TCGA oral ratios, as well as pseudocount (log₂((T + pc)/(N + pc)) for pc ∈ {1.0, 0.1, 0.01}), linear fold, and basally expressed subsets (detection ≥ 0.05 and mean ≥ 0.05 CP10K). Unbiased HPA (155 cell types) and GTEx (54 tissues) scans were multiplicity-corrected using Benjamini–Hochberg FDR across both payload-adjusted Tobit LR and Spearman correlations (Table S4). Out-of-sample calibration used prespecified LOTO (implemented with zero-antigen-overlap folds holding out all training ADCs sharing any target antigen with the held-out ADC, excluding *EGFR*;*ERBB3* alongside both *EGFR* and *ERBB3* ADCs) and exploratory 44-fold LOO-ADC (per-ADC predictions in Table S6a), comparing predicted expit(Xβ) × 100 against payload-only models via distribution-free left-censored concordance (C-index across all 451 unambiguously ordered pairs, evaluated with 5,000 target-level LOTO refit permutations), exact Mann–Whitney ROC-AUC and step-function PR-AUC on exact uncensored binary toxicity endpoints (≥10% across 35/44 ADCs, ≥15% across 38/44 ADCs, and ≥20% across 44/44 ADCs where censoring thresholds ≤ cutoff confirm exact negative status; Table S6b), Spearman ρ and MAE on numeric ADCs, exact two-sided McNemar tests on censored threshold placement, and exploratory out-of-fold Tobit log-scores (Table S6c). Statistical modeling used Python (SciPy, Virtanen et al., 2020; statsmodels, Seabold and Perktold, 2010) and visualization used R (R Core Team, 2025; ggplot2, Wickham, 2016; ggrepel, Slowikowski, 2026; cowplot, Wilke, 2025).

### OpenFDA FAERS Pharmacovigilance Analysis

Adverse-event counts were queried from openFDA FAERS endpoints (Kass-Hout et al., 2016; queried November 16, 2026) across 15 ADCs (12 unique targets) with ≥50 reports excluding 69 co-reported antineoplastic and anti-HER2 drugs (openFDA AND NOT exclusion without separate drug-characterization suspect-role filtering). Oral events encompassed 7 MedDRA preferred terms (stomatitis, mucosal inflammation, mouth ulceration, aphthous ulcer, glossitis, oral mucosal erythema, oral mucosal blistering), compared against prespecified neutropenia and peripheral neuropathy controls plus four exploratory toxicity controls. Because report denominators spanned 67 to 13,081 reports per drug, payload-adjusted disproportionality was modeled using overdispersed negative-binomial (NB2) maximum-likelihood count regression with exact log(n reports) exposure offset (fitted via bounded L-BFGS-B with analytic gradients), reporting Wald P values, df = 1 likelihood-ratio tests, and 10,000 target-level LR permutations across the 12 unique targets (seed = 1), alongside unadjusted Spearman correlations, equal-weight empirical log-odds OLS (log((k + 0.5)/(n − k + 0.5)) ~ payload class + basal; evaluated under the identical 10,000-resample 12-target likelihood-ratio permutation) sensitivity checks, and pairwise reporting odds ratios (Table S8).

### Surfaceome Curation, OSCC Re-Ranking, and Prediction Intervals

Human reviewed UniProtKB/Swiss-Prot entries (20,429 proteins, downloaded November 17, 2026; UniProt Consortium, 2025) possessing transmembrane helices or GPI anchors alongside signal peptide or cell-membrane annotation (excluding intracellular-organelle-only and primary endoplasmic reticulum/Golgi/mitochondrial/nuclear/peroxisomal/cytoplasmic entries) yielded 3,152 surfaceome genes (38/38 clinical ADC targets), of which 1,997 were measured in all three scRNA cohorts (after harmonizing legacy symbol *PVRL4* to *NECTIN4*) and 420 were detected in ≥25% of malignant cells in both Choi and Puram. Exploratory four-criterion prioritization selected targets with (1) ≥50% detection in both OSCC cohorts, (2) predicted topoisomerase-I stomatitis ≤12%, (3) TCGA-HNSC oral paired LFC > 0 with ≥60% pairs up, and (4) CPTAC-HNSCC oral TMT paired protein LFC > 0 with ≥50% pairs up (n ≥ 15 pairs). Per-target 95% confidence intervals for the conditional median (from 2,000 target-cluster bootstrap parameter draws, seed = 1) and new-ADC 95% prediction intervals (adding Gaussian Tobit residual draws, seed = 42) were computed uniformly from the full 44-ADC fit, with separate zero-antigen-overlap LOTO estimates and intervals reported for in-training targets alongside train-max extrapolation flags (>2.29 log₂ units) and full 54-tissue GTEx maxima in Table S7. Threshold robustness was tallied across 3 × 3 exploratory grids (10%/12%/15% stomatitis × 10/15/30 TPM GTEx max, with criteria 1, 3, and 4 held fixed so that targets failing any of these score 0/9) under both the four-organ core panel (heart left ventricle, liver, kidney cortex, tibial nerve) and an extended 24-organ panel adding all 13 GTEx brain regions plus lung, stomach, transverse colon, terminal ileum, pancreas, spleen, and whole blood.
